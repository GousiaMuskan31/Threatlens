The problem

Your tools each see a piece. Attackers use the whole picture.

Security teams stitch together partial visibility from a dozen consoles while alert volume grows faster than headcount.

01

Fragmented telemetry

Every tool has its own data model, console and query language. One login investigation can mean pivoting across six interfaces.

02

Alert fatigue

Low-fidelity alerts and duplicates bury genuine incidents, and analysts can't investigate everything.

03

Analyst shortage

Understaffed SOCs and repetitive triage drive burnout and turnover.

04

Long dwell time

Stolen credentials and living-off-the-land attacks slip past signatures for weeks.

FAQ

Questions, answered

Does ThreatLens replace my SIEM or EDR?

No. It sits above your existing tools, ingests their telemetry and adds correlation and behavioral analytics no single tool can perform alone.

How is it deployed?

Three options: managed SaaS (days to onboard), dedicated single-tenant cloud (1–2 weeks), or self-hosted for air-gapped and highly regulated environments.

Can I trust automated findings?

Every finding keeps its underlying evidence and top contributing factors, so analysts can verify why it was flagged. Automation is analyst-in-the-loop by default.

How long until I see value?

Implementation is phased: discovery and sizing, core onboarding in 2–4 weeks, then tuning and expansion. Expect a tuning period while behavioral baselines learn your environment.

Can MSSPs use it?

Yes. A partner program includes multi-tenant management tooling for teams monitoring many client environments.
